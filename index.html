Scorpion-Inspired Corrugated Surfaces for Martian Rovers
to Prevent Dust Accumulation and Dust-induced Erosion

@ Unmanned Exploration Laboratory (Seoul, Korea)

May 2023 - June 2023

Background

→

Dust tend to accumulate on the surfaces of rovers due to the Martian winds, resulting in surface erosion and failures of sensitive equipment.

Curiosity Rover on MarsImage courtesy of NASA, JPL, and the Planetary Society

Desert animals on earth, such as scorpions, undergo similar kinds of problem, but have been countering them by introducing grooves in their joints that create small pools of turbulent air throughout the evolution.

Objective

This summer project at Unmanned Exploration Laboratory was conducted to see how such grooves and induced eddy airflow will assist in prevention of dust on Martian rovers.

Methods

We know that from Zhiwu et al. [1] a vortex will form due to an external flow right above the grooves of the surface, on Earth.

To find out whether such grooves are also applicable to Martian rovers, we thus need to examine how strong these vortices would be on Mars.

That is, we need to calculate the unknown magnitudes of:

velocity u and w

governed by both geometric and environmental factors:

external flow velocity (U)
groove height (h)
groove length (l)
gravitational constant (g)
viscosity (μ)
density (ρ)

Results

Going through series of calculations involve the continuity equation, the Navier-Stokes equations, and a preassumed velocity shape of w, giving out the following amplitude:

\( w(x,z) = \alpha \sin\!\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{l}x\right)\cos^2\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{h}z\right) \)
\( -\dfrac{\partial p'}{\partial x} + \mu\left(\dfrac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \dfrac{\partial^2 u}{\partial z^2}\right) = \rho\left(u\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} + w\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right) \)
\( \alpha = -\dfrac{5U\,l^2 h}{2\pi^2\,(h^2+l^2)\,(h+l)} \)
\( \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \dfrac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0 \)
\( -\dfrac{\partial p'}{\partial z} + \mu\left(\dfrac{\partial^2 w}{\partial x^2} + \dfrac{\partial^2 w}{\partial z^2}\right) = \rho\left(u\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial x} + w\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial z}\right) \)
\( \alpha = -\dfrac{5}{8\pi^2}U \qquad (l = h = r) \)

The following is a simulated formation of vortices on the corrugated surface, with values r = 5mm, U = 6m/s, and Martian values for density and viscosity.

The vortices clearly form in the picture, with amplitude values around 0.5m/s, which closely matches the derived value from the equation, 0.36 m/s.

This result shows that these corrugated surfaces can be applied to Martian rovers to reduce dust accumulation and erosion, preventing equipment malfunction and increasing durability.
